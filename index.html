How the numbers are computed

kWh/month = watts × quantity × hours per day × days per month ÷ 1000
Cost = kWh × your rate (₱/kWh)  ·  CO₂ = kWh × grid emission factor (kg CO₂/kWh)

Results are estimates. Actual use depends on appliance age, settings and how often motors such as compressors run.

Energy, electricity and climate

Short readings on the ideas behind WattWise.

Watts versus kilowatt-hours

A watt (W) measures power, how fast an appliance uses energy at a given moment. A kilowatt-hour (kWh) measures energy, power used over time. A 1,000 W flat iron used for one hour uses 1 kWh, and so does a 10 W LED bulb left on for 100 hours. Your electric meter counts kWh, and you are billed per kWh.

Where your electricity's carbon comes from

Much of the Philippines' electricity comes from power plants that burn coal, oil or natural gas, which release carbon dioxide (CO₂). Hydropower, geothermal, solar and wind produce little or none. The more fossil fuel in the grid's mix, the more CO₂ each kWh carries.

What a grid emission factor is

The grid emission factor is the average CO₂ released per unit of electricity on a grid. The Department of Energy publishes it for the Luzon-Visayas and Mindanao grids. WattWise uses the DOE's 2015–2017 Mindanao figure of 0.7797 tonnes CO₂ per MWh, which equals about 0.78 kg per kWh.

Standby or "phantom" power

Many devices keep drawing power when switched off but still plugged in: TVs with remotes, set-top boxes, chargers, microwaves with clocks. Each draws only a few watts, but they draw it 24 hours a day. Unplugging them or using a power strip with a switch stops this waste.

Inverter technology

A non-inverter aircon or refrigerator runs its compressor at full speed, then switches off and on to hold the temperature. An inverter model adjusts its compressor speed instead, avoiding repeated full-power starts. In normal use this saves a large share of energy, which is why inverter appliances cost more upfront but less to run.

Energy efficiency and the law

The Energy Efficiency and Conservation Act (Republic Act 11285, 2019) makes energy efficiency a national policy and requires energy labels on appliances such as aircons and refrigerators. When buying, compare the label's efficiency rating and estimated yearly kWh, not only the price.

Reading your electric bill

Your bill shows your previous and present meter readings; the difference is the kWh you used. The total amount includes generation, transmission, distribution, system loss, taxes and other charges. Dividing the total amount by kWh used gives your effective rate per kWh, which is the rate to enter in WattWise.

Why small savings add up

Cutting 30 kWh a month in one home avoids about 23 kg of CO₂ (30 × 0.78). Across 1,000 homes in a city, that becomes about 23 tonnes every month. Lower household demand also eases strain on the grid during peak hours.
